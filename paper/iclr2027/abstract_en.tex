On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers reasoning behavior from a teacher to a student through dense feedback on student-generated trajectories. Common sampled-token implementations assign a separate feedback signal to each generated token, although a local reasoning decision may be expressed across several tokens. Can sharing feedback within a short span improve this learning process? We introduce Block3 Mean, which averages teacher--student log-probability differences over three adjacent tokens and combines the shared signal with a joint block importance ratio and a valid-length-weighted objective. The method uses existing teacher scores without an additional reward model or process annotations. We analyze its update dynamics and evaluate mathematical reasoning across teacher--student pairs and saved checkpoints. In the historical Qwen3-1.7B comparison at Step200, Block3 improves Avg@8 over sampled-token OPD by 8.90 percentage points on MATH500 and 5.42 on AMC23. The official Qwen3-8B-to-1.7B Instruct pair also has positive Step200 Avg@8 differences on all four benchmarks. Our analysis connects these comparisons to local credit sharing, gradient scale and clipping geometry, and provides diagnostics that distinguish numerical stability from generation quality. Together, the method, experiments and analysis offer an approach to studying local credit assignment in reasoning distillation.
