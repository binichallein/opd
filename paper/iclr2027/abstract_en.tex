On-policy distillation provides teacher feedback at student-generated prefixes, but the appropriate granularity of this feedback remains unclear. We study Block3 Mean, which shares averaged teacher--student log-probability differences across three adjacent tokens. Source analysis reveals a joint block importance ratio and a different loss reduction: equal loss values can coexist with threefold policy-loss derivatives at the old policy. The intervention is therefore not pure signal smoothing. We report matched Qwen3-4B Base and official Qwen3-8B-to-1.7B Instruct comparisons alongside earlier pairs, window variants and failures. At Step200, the 4B comparison favors Token on three of four mean-accuracy benchmarks. The Instruct pair instead has Block3 Avg@8 differences of $+0.10$, $+2.50$, $+0.83$ and $+0.30$ percentage points, but all paired-question intervals include zero and Pass@8 is mixed. Both methods improve its original student's mean accuracy, yet reduce MATH500 eight-attempt coverage. Earlier small-model failures and finite-gradient generation collapse further limit generalization. These single-seed results characterize conditional benefits and confounds, motivating separate control of advantage aggregation, importance-ratio granularity and gradient scale before claiming a generally better distillation method.
