On-policy distillation provides teacher feedback at student-generated prefixes, but the appropriate granularity of this feedback remains unclear. We study Block3 Mean, which shares averaged teacher--student log-probability differences across three adjacent tokens. A source-level analysis shows that the evaluated implementation also introduces a joint block importance ratio and a different loss reduction: even equal advantages can produce equal loss values but threefold policy-loss derivatives at the old policy. These changes prevent interpreting the method as pure signal smoothing. We combine a matched Qwen3-4B completion-protocol comparison with an audit of earlier model pairs, window variants, block-size sweeps and failed runs. Historical 1.7B improvements do not transfer consistently. The current 4B comparison exhibits intermediate gains and checkpoint-dependent reversals, with Step200 favoring Token OPD on three of four mean-accuracy benchmarks; smaller Qwen and Llama students exhibit substantial negative outcomes. Finite gradients do not exclude generation collapse. Our results characterize the benefits, confounds and limits of local credit sharing, and motivate separately controlling advantage aggregation, importance-ratio granularity and gradient scale before claiming a generally better distillation method.
