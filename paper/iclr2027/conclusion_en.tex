Block-level distillation changes more than the number of tokens summarized by a teacher. In the implementation studied here, it changes where credit is assigned, which policy deviations are clipped, and how policy-loss derivatives are scaled. Historical 1.7B gains do not justify a universal claim: other pairs show mixed results or severe degradation, while current 4B intermediate gains reverse across checkpoints and its endpoint favors Token on most mean-accuracy benchmarks. The practical lesson is to audit the exact objective and the generated trajectories together. Local advantage sharing remains a testable hypothesis; isolating it from ratio and normalization changes is the necessary next experiment, rather than a benefit already proved by these results.
